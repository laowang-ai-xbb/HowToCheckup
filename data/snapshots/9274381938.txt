 Turning Evidence Into Action

 A watershed moment in health care

 From 2012 to 2023, the ABIM Foundation&#x27;s Choosing Wisely campaign encouraged conversations between clinicians and patients about avoiding unnecessary tests and treatments. What began as an effort to reduce overuse became a global movement that influenced health care policy, clinical practice, medical education and patient care.

 Choosing Wisely demonstrated how shared knowledge, practical tools, and professional leadership can change how clinicians and patients think about care.

 A Global Movement
 Starting conversations about unnecessary care
 Choosing Wisely launched in 2012 with nine national specialty societies representing 375,000 clinicians. Together, they recommended reducing the use of 45 tests or treatments that lacked strong supporting evidence. By 2023, more than 80 specialty societies had contributed more than 700 recommendations highlighting overused or unnecessary tests and treatments.

 While the ABIM Foundation no longer maintains these recommendations, we encourage interested specialty societies to continue to make their recommendations available. 
 From Knowledge to Practice 
 Giving clinicians and patients tools to make informed decisions together
 Choosing Wisely translated evidence into practical resources that clinicians and patients could use in conversations about care. Its recommendations gave them a shared starting point for considering whether a test or treatment was necessary, and educational resources helped bring those conversations into clinical care.

 Programs such as Choosing Wisely STARS, along with training modules and simulation tools, helped students and clinicians build the knowledge and communication skills needed to navigate conversations about unnecessary care.
 Choosing Wisely also contributed to a growing body of research about overuse and how to address it. The campaign has been referenced in more than 300 academic journal articles examining topics ranging from patient-clinician communication to interventions designed to reduce low-value care. This research has helped extend the campaign’s influence beyond individual conversations and build knowledge about how clinicians and health care organizations can address unnecessary care.

 A Lasting Legacy 
 Carrying the lessons forward
 Choosing Wisely showed what can happen when the profession comes together around a shared challenge. It helped make unnecessary care part of the national conversation and gave clinicians and patients a framework for making thoughtful decisions together.
 Although the ABIM Foundation concluded the campaign in 2023, its lessons continue to inform our work today. Choosing Wisely demonstrated the power of professional leadership, shared knowledge, and patient-clinician partnership to improve care. Those lessons continue to shape how the Foundation advances medical professionalism to build trust and improve care.
 From unnecessary care to medical debt
 Choosing Wisely&#x27;s primary goal was to help patients avoid care that could do more harm than good. We also recognized that reducing unnecessary tests and treatments could ease the financial burden on patients and contribute to a more effective and affordable health care system.
 In the years since we launched the campaign, affordability has become an increasingly salient issue in American health care. Patients today face extraordinary challenges in paying for their care, with about 40 percent of American adults currently carrying medical and/or dental debt. These patients often delay or skip care, experience worse health outcomes, and lose trust in the health care system. This makes it far more challenging for clinicians to meet their professional responsibility to provide appropriate care that keeps their patients healthy.
 Building on the lessons of Choosing Wisely, the Foundation has sought to address the medical debt problem by convening experts to discuss potential solutions and then partnering with AcademyHealth to create a playbook that includes practical advice for hospital leaders and clinicians who want to help patients avoid falling any further into debt.
